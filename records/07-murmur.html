The Review

The first R.E.M. song I heard was “Radio Free Europe” on a community station whose signal faded whenever the weather turned. Michael Stipe’s words were blurred even before the static reached them, but the mystery made the song more inviting. I found Murmur at a record fair several years later and was surprised by how quietly confident the whole album sounded. Peter Buck’s guitar chimes without becoming decorative, and the rhythm section does more of the heavy lifting than casual listeners notice. “Perfect Circle” is the song I return to most; it feels like remembering a place that may never have existed. At first I thought the record’s hazy production made everything too similar. Over time the differences emerged, like objects becoming visible in a dark room. It rewards attention without demanding admiration, which may be why it has survived so many changes in my listening habits.
