The Review

I bought this on release week after months of magazine photographs, rumours, and arguments about whether the band could possibly follow Appetite. The first answer was apparently to release nearly everything. Use Your Illusion I is messy, overlong, and incapable of walking past a piano without turning it into a grand statement. I mean most of that affectionately. “November Rain” is the song I return to because it commits completely to its own scale; the final guitar passage still earns the dramatic journey. “Coma” is exhausting but impressive, and “Dust N’ Bones” shows the looser band that might have made a more manageable album. Several tracks could have used a firm editor, and I rarely play all four vinyl sides in one sitting. Still, the sprawl documents a band refusing to remain inside the dirty club where everyone first loved them. My copy’s lyric sleeves are nearly worn through from trying to follow every detour.
