The Review

Someone played the title track at a party in 1987, and the room divided neatly between people who wanted it louder and people who wanted to leave. I belonged to the first group. I bought the cassette the following week and nearly wore out the opening of “Battery” by rewinding it for friends. What astonished me was not simply the speed. These songs are constructed with the patience of progressive rock, only every change arrives with physical force. “Master of Puppets” remains the peak: the central quiet passage makes the return of the main riff feel even heavier. “Orion” proved that the band could tell a story without a singer, and it made Cliff Burton’s death seem more real to a young listener far away. The album can be exhausting, and I need the right mood for its relentless precision, but it has never sounded foolish or dated to me.
