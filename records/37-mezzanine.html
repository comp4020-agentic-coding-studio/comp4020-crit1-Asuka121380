The Review

I first heard “Angel” in a darkened hi-fi shop while comparing speakers I could not afford. The bass seemed to begin below the floor, and by the time the guitar arrived the polite demonstration room felt genuinely threatening. I left without the speakers but with Mezzanine. This is electronic music with the weight and physical unease I had always sought in heavy rock. “Teardrop” is my favourite because Elizabeth Fraser’s voice introduces warmth without dissolving the tension; the heartbeat rhythm holds everything at the edge of sleep. “Inertia Creeps” feels closer and more claustrophobic, while “Group Four” builds with frightening patience. The album’s mood is so consistent that I rarely choose individual tracks, though it can darken an afternoon more than intended. Its production is detailed without becoming a showroom exercise. I have used this CD to test every stereo since, but the songs always outlast the equipment discussion.
