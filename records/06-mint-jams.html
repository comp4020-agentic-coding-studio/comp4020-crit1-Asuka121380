The Review

I first heard CASIOPEA on a cassette copied by a student who had returned from Japan. The handwriting on the label was nearly impossible to read, so for months I did not know whether CASIOPEA was the band, the album, or the name of the shop. The playing was dazzling, but unlike some fusion records it never felt like four musicians arguing over who could fit in the most notes. Mint Jams moves with the clean excitement of a night drive through an unfamiliar city. “Domino Line” is my favourite because the bass and keyboards keep pushing forward while the guitar stays bright and conversational. “Asayake” is the tune I use to convince rock listeners that polish does not have to mean softness. I finally found the CD through an import catalogue and paid far too much postage. The glossy case remains one of the few objects on this shelf I handle carefully.
