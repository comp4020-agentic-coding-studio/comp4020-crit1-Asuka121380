The Review

A British music paper described Oasis with such unreasonable certainty that I bought the album partly to argue with it. “Rock ’n’ Roll Star” began, the guitars filled every available space, and the argument was postponed. Definitely Maybe is not subtle, but it understands how belief can become a musical instrument. “Live Forever” remains my favourite because beneath the bravado is a remarkably generous idea: survival imagined as a communal chorus. “Slide Away” is the song that proves the band could stretch beyond slogans, while “Supersonic” still walks into a room like it owns the furniture. The dense guitar sound becomes tiring near the end, and Liam Gallagher’s posture is easier to admire in three-minute doses than in interviews. None of that cancels the record’s momentum. I bought my copy before the band became unavoidable and therefore preserve the collector’s ridiculous privilege of claiming that it sounded better when fewer people knew it.
