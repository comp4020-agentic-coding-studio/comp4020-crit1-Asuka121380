The Review

A British traveller left a copied tape of this album at a share house after insisting that Manchester had already replaced every important city in music. His claim was unbearable, but the tape was excellent. The guitars shimmered rather than attacked, and the rhythm section carried a dance impulse that made ordinary rock timing seem stiff. I eventually bought the CD and still enjoy the slow entrance of “I Wanna Be Adored,” but “I Am the Resurrection” is the real destination. It begins as a sharp, arrogant song and then opens into a long instrumental release that never feels like padding. Some of the lyrics confuse confidence with greatness, and history may not support every boast. For one album, however, the band sounds capable of proving almost anything. The cover’s paint and lemons have faded slightly in my front window, which is entirely my fault and now part of the object’s story.
