The Review

I bought this for my father and then borrowed it so often that he eventually told me to keep it. The title track is an easy invitation: two famous guitarists trading lines without turning the session into an athletic contest. B.B. King’s economy is the lesson throughout. He can make one bent note carry more personality than a younger player’s entire solo, while Clapton sounds happiest when answering rather than dominating. “Ten Long Years” has the relaxed authority of musicians who no longer need to rush toward the chorus. The production is polished and occasionally too comfortable; I would welcome a little more club-floor dirt around the edges. Even so, the warmth is genuine, and the record connects the blues foundation of my rock collection to the cleaner sound of the present. There is now a second copy in my father’s house. I suspect he bought it mainly to stop asking for his first one back.
