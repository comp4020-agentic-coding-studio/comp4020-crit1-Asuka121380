The Review

I found Tutu because a guitarist in a local band insisted that anyone interested in electric music should listen to Miles Davis, then handed me this rather than one of the famous older records. The programmed drums and glossy keyboards initially sounded too clean beside the rough rock albums I loved. Then Miles entered on the title track and seemed to scratch a human line across the polished surface. His trumpet does not compete with the machinery; it makes the machinery feel strange. “Portia” is the piece I play late at night, while “Tutu” still has the strongest shape and attitude. I would not pretend this is the only Miles album anyone needs, and some of the production stamps the date very clearly. That is part of its fascination. It connects jazz improvisation to the studio technology of its moment and helped prepare my ears for electronic records that arrived a decade later.
