The Review

I discovered Neutral Milk Hotel through a photocopied fanzine review that spent more time describing tape hiss than the songs. That sounded unpromising, but the writer’s enthusiasm persuaded me to order On Avery Island from a small label catalogue. “Song Against Sex” arrived like a badly tuned parade, and Jeff Mangum’s voice seemed far too large for the recording equipment. By “Naomi,” the roughness had become part of the emotional language. That is still my favourite: yearning, melodic, and just unstable enough to avoid sweetness. The album wanders near the end, and the long closing noise is something I admire more often than I enjoy. Yet the best songs feel handmade in the truest sense, not as a fashionable claim to authenticity. The booklet and thin case seemed almost homemade too. This record taught me to stop treating imperfect sound as a barrier; sometimes the distortion is where the singer leaves the fingerprints.
