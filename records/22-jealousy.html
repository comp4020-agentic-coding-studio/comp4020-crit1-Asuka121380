The Review

After Blue Blood, I expected X Japan to become even faster and louder. Jealousy does contain the furious “Silent Jealousy,” but it also feels more controlled, as if the band learned that drama becomes larger when not every curtain rises at once. I first heard the song on a tape sent by an acquaintance in Osaka, recorded from a radio broadcast with the announcer speaking over the final seconds. The proper CD took months to arrive through mail order. “Silent Jealousy” remains the favourite for its piano opening, racing rhythm, and melody that refuses to disappear beneath the speed. “Say Anything” is unapologetically sentimental, while “Voiceless Screaming” offers a welcome acoustic change of colour. A few stylistic turns sit awkwardly together, but that is part of following this band. They make genre boundaries look like stage scenery: useful for a moment, then pushed aside for the next scene.
