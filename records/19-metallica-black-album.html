The Review

The black cassette appeared in every car and workshop I entered in 1991. Some older metal fans complained that Metallica had slowed down, but the first drum hits of “Enter Sandman” sounded heavier to me because there was room around them. I bought my copy at a shopping-centre music store and played it until the lettering began rubbing off the shell. “The Unforgiven” is now my favourite: the reversed idea of a heavy verse and cleaner chorus gives the song a tired dignity that suits its subject. “Wherever I May Roam” remains tremendous driving music, while “Nothing Else Matters” works best when nobody is arguing about whether a metal band is allowed to write it. The production is almost too perfect, and the second half contains fewer surprises than Master of Puppets. Even so, this record understands the physical pleasure of a single guitar note arriving at exactly the right weight.
