The Review

The first thing I heard was the opening of “Welcome to the Jungle” coming from a builder’s radio across the street. It sounded dangerous in a way most polished late-eighties rock only pretended to be. I traded two Iron Maiden tapes for Appetite for Destruction, a decision that felt reckless for about ten minutes and correct ever since. “Sweet Child o’ Mine” has the famous guitar figure, but “Rocket Queen” is my favourite because it gathers the whole album’s sleaze, rhythm, and surprising vulnerability into one ending. “Mr. Brownstone” also swings harder than many metal bands would permit themselves. The record’s behaviour is frequently ugly, and I do not admire every word simply because the guitars are excellent. Still, the band sounds hungry rather than manufactured. My cassette is stretched near the end of side one, yet replacing it would erase the history of all those hot afternoons when it lived in my car.
