The Review

I brought Kid A home expecting guitars and received a voice dissolving into “Everything in Its Right Place.” My first reaction was annoyance, followed closely by curiosity. The band had not abandoned emotion; it had hidden emotion inside unfamiliar surfaces and asked the listener to come looking. “How to Disappear Completely” is my favourite because the acoustic guitar and floating strings give the album a human centre without explaining away its fear. “The National Anthem” turns bass and brass into a traffic collision, while “Idioteque” achieves more panic with electronics than many metal bands manage with amplifiers. I do miss the direct physical sound of The Bends in places, and some interludes feel more like corridors than rooms. But corridors matter when an album is designed as a building. This disc changed my understanding of what a successful rock band was permitted to do after the audience had learned its language.
