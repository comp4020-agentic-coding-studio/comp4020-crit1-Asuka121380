The Review

I heard “Smells Like Teen Spirit” from a television in the back room of a pub and watched conversations stop one by one. The song was noisy, catchy, and strangely exhausted at the same time. I bought Nevermind the next morning, expecting the rest to be rougher, and was surprised by how carefully the melodies had been built. “Come as You Are” is my favourite because its watery guitar and uncertain invitation capture the band’s tension without needing to explode immediately. “Drain You” may be the cleverest pop song on the record, while “Something in the Way” leaves the room colder than it found it. The polished sound occasionally makes the anger too easy to consume, a criticism that In Utero later seemed to share. That does not reduce the songs. My jewel case has a crack across the baby photograph, acquired when somebody stepped on it during a house move, and I have never replaced it.
