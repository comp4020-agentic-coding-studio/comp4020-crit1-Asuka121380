The Review

A school friend’s older brother played me “Elephant Talk” because he thought the guitar noises would annoy me. Instead, I made him start the side again. I eventually bought Discipline from a narrow shop where the progressive-rock section smelled permanently of dust and cardboard. What keeps bringing me back is the tension between machinery and personality. “Frame by Frame” sounds as if two precise systems are slipping past one another, yet it never becomes a cold exercise. “Matte Kudasai” gives the record a welcome patch of open sky, while “Indiscipline” remains funny, threatening, and completely committed to its own nervous breakdown. I admit that this is not an album I play for every visitor; some people hear the interlocking guitars and immediately look tired. For me, the effort is part of the pleasure. It taught me that virtuosity can create anxiety and texture, not merely long solos.
