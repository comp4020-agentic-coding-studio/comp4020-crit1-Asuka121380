The Review

A drummer friend brought this album to rehearsal and used “Give It Away” as evidence that every rock bassist should learn restraint by first learning how not to be restrained. The record sounded loose and immediate, as if the band had taken over a house rather than entered a laboratory. I expected the funk and noise; I did not expect “I Could Have Lied,” which remains my favourite because John Frusciante’s guitar seems to answer each uncomfortable sentence. “Under the Bridge” has suffered from being everywhere, but played in the album’s sequence it still feels exposed and necessary. At over seventy minutes, the record asks more patience than every experiment deserves, and some of the humour has not improved with age. Even so, the best performances breathe in a way that expensive early-nineties rock often did not. My copy was borrowed repeatedly, and the booklet came back with three different coffee stains and no confession.
