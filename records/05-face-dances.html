The Review

I came to this record backwards. Everyone around me spoke about Who’s Next, but in 1982 the album I could actually afford in the bargain bin was Face Dances. Without Keith Moon it inevitably feels more careful, and I understand why older fans were disappointed. Still, “You Better You Bet” caught me immediately: big synthesiser, bigger chorus, and Roger Daltrey singing as if disappointment itself could be shouted into submission. “Another Tricky Day” has become the track I turn to when ordinary life feels needlessly complicated. The weaker songs do not have the reckless spark of the classic records, but I have affection for the sound of a great band trying to discover what it can be after a loss. The portrait-grid cover is worn white along the top edge, possibly from the previous owner’s shelf. It is not the definitive Who album, but it is the one that became mine.
