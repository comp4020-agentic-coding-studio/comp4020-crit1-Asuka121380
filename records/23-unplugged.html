The Review

I heard the rearranged “Layla” on the radio while painting a spare room and nearly missed the announcement because I did not recognise it. The old song had lost its speed and gained the weary humour of someone examining a youthful disaster from a safer distance. I bought Unplugged expecting that novelty and ended up staying for the quieter performances. “Old Love” is my favourite because the extended guitar work feels conversational rather than competitive. “Tears in Heaven” is difficult to discuss without reducing private grief to a review, so I will only say that its restraint matters. Not every blues standard here is transformed, and the polite audience sometimes makes the event feel more respectable than the music requires. Still, the record reminded many electric-guitar owners that tone begins in the hands. My CD became Sunday-morning music, often played while repairing sleeves and reorganising the cabinet after a louder Saturday night.
