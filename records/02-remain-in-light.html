The Review

I bought this after hearing “Once in a Lifetime” on late-night radio and wondering whether the announcer had accidentally played two records at once. The rhythm seemed to move in several directions while David Byrne sounded like a worried preacher trying to remember why he had entered the room. I was used to rock bands being organised around a heroic guitar, so this record rearranged the furniture in my head. “Born Under Punches” and “Crosseyed and Painless” are even stranger: nervous, crowded, and somehow precise. My copy came from a university book sale in the late eighties, filed incorrectly among comedy records, which still seems appropriate. The second side loses a little of the first side’s impossible momentum, but “The Overload” has grown on me as the room-darkening conclusion. It is one of those albums that made later electronic and experimental records easier for me to understand.
