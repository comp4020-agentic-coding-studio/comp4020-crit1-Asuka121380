The Review

I bought The Division Bell partly from loyalty and partly because the enormous metal heads on the cover looked magnificent in the shop display. Nobody should begin exploring Pink Floyd here, but older collections are not built only from sensible starting points. The album is spacious, beautifully recorded, and occasionally so comfortable that it risks drifting past without leaving a mark. Then David Gilmour bends a note and the room remembers why it is listening. “High Hopes” is the essential track: bells, memories, regret, and a final guitar solo that earns the album’s widescreen scale. “Marooned” works well late at night, while “Coming Back to Life” is more direct than its surroundings. I understand complaints that the record lacks Roger Waters’ friction. On the right evening, however, I value its patience. My copy was a birthday present and still contains a small card, which gives its nostalgia an additional private layer.
