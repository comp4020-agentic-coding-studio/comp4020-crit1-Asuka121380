The Review

For years I dismissed synthesiser bands as people pressing buttons while rock musicians did the real work. Violator corrected that foolishness. A colleague played “Personal Jesus” from a promotional cassette, and the dry guitar riff was familiar enough to get me through the door. Behind it I found an album where every electronic sound has weight, space, and purpose. “Enjoy the Silence” is my favourite because its melody seems simple until the arrangement begins revealing small moving parts around it. “Waiting for the Night” turns the room blue, while “Clean” closes the record with admirable patience. I still prefer a live drummer on many days, and the severe mood can become a little too immaculate if I play the album repeatedly. Yet this is not cold music. Dave Gahan’s voice and Martin Gore’s songs keep desire and unease close to the surface. It gave me a bridge from guitars to the electronic records that followed.
